# Cognitive Trails: review and pilot proposal

Reviewed September 4, 2026 against the current working tree, including uncommitted changes. This is a proposal for discussion, not an agreed product change. No implementation files were changed during this review.

## Assessment

Cognitive Trails has a substantive foundation: passive capture, local inference, persistent topic identity, incremental processing, provenance, and a testable architecture. Its most promising benefit is helping someone recover and continue an evolving line of inquiry without remembering to bookmark or organize every page.

The main unresolved product question is whether people want this primarily to understand their browsing patterns or to recover useful context. My recommendation is to test recovery and resumption as the first practical benefit, while retaining reflection as a secondary experience. This is a hypothesis, not evidence of demand.

A candidate promise: **Pick up a line of thought where you left it. Cognitive Trails privately keeps the pages and browsing sessions that help you return.**

The first audience should be a group we can actually observe using it: five people who repeatedly research subjects across multiple sessions. Researchers, writers, developers, and students are possible sources of participants; choose one workflow rather than trying to serve all of them at launch. For this proposal, “real” means a small group uses it voluntarily each week.

## Product critique

### Put the useful material first

The current home screen leads with Spread, Continuity, and Active rings. It then repeats these metrics in a baseline card. Topic names in the weekly summary, daily activities, and brief are rendered as text instead of links to their supporting pages.

An initial home could offer:

1. Search your trails, initially over titles, topic labels, domains, and dates.
2. A short list of recent trails, each with its latest session, representative pages, and a clear Open trail action.
3. Changes in the record, with every topic observation linked to evidence.
4. Browsing-pattern statistics behind a secondary view.

The existing map can remain available for exploration. A chronological page list should be sufficient to complete the first recovery task without learning the graph.

### A topic and a trail serve different purposes

“Async Rust Programming” is a topic. A sequence of documentation, an example, and a discussion visited while working through a problem is a browsing episode. A person's longer line of inquiry can include several topics and episodes.

Use existing topic IDs and visit records to expose dated episodes before adding another inference layer. Show observed ordering and membership without claiming that one page caused a thought or that the system knows the user's objective. User-written trail names or notes could later add meaning explicitly. Optional corrections should be available in context; they need not become a review queue.

### Keep neutrality, make room for agency

The refusal to grade people or infer their goals is worth preserving. Users can still reopen a page, pin a trail, rename a topic, hide an observation, or explicitly join material they regard as connected.

Neutral language alone does not guarantee neutral presentation. The Active ring becomes full at eight hours; its appearance can suggest a completion target. “3/5 metrics within your range” can similarly look like a score. Consider simple values and historical distributions if reflection remains central.

### Narrow the measurement claim

The extension observes browser activity and estimates dwell. That is useful evidence, but it is not a complete measure of attention or cognition. Silent reading, video, other applications, other devices, uncaptured content, and classification mistakes all limit interpretation.

The home screen currently says “Active” and “active” without displaying the estimate method or categorized coverage. It contains no coverage rendering, despite the documentation's commitment to show coverage beside claims. Add a small, comprehensible coverage and freshness statement near any statistics. A lack of observations should remain distinguishable from a claim that an interest disappeared.

### Shorten time to first benefit

The current setup requires Ollama, two model downloads, developer-mode extension loading, and a run before the record becomes useful. Baseline comparisons additionally require 14 comparable days. Historical titles can support an initial record, but importing history cannot reconstruct page text and measured active time from before installation.

For a technical pilot, assisted installation is acceptable. Before broader distribution, add setup checks, model/download progress, a clear first-run state, and a useful experience when inference is unavailable. Measure actual installation and first-result time rather than assuming setup is straightforward.

Benchmark smaller local models before choosing a new runtime. Chrome's built-in Prompt API is a candidate for generation, not a replacement for the entire embedding and registry pipeline; its documented hardware, storage, and availability requirements still need evaluation. Preserve local processing. [Chrome Prompt API documentation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/prompt-api)

### Differentiate through continuity

Recall documents content capture, summaries, automatically linked content, and resurfacing connections during browsing. MemexNG's store listing advertises local semantic browsing-history search. These establish adjacent feature offerings, not comparative product quality or market demand. [Recall documentation](https://docs.recall.it/), [MemexNG listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/memexng-semantic-history/pbmabnkilngleagchdopehchhhhbpail)

Consequently, “local AI memory” alone is an insufficient differentiation hypothesis. Cognitive Trails should test whether the history of returning to and moving through subjects is more useful than finding one isolated page.

## Current engineering findings

### Confirmed: page identity collapses distinct content

`lib/text.js:36` strips all query parameters and fragments. A direct check mapped both `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=videoA` and `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=videoB` to `https://youtube.com/watch`. Pages addressed through query parameters or hash routes have the same issue.

Because this identity keys pages and memberships, distinct content can share one page record and topic assignment. Define page identity separately from URL sanitization: retain known content identifiers and route identity while stripping tracking and secrets. Verify distinct videos remain distinct and tracking-only variants still deduplicate.

### Confirmed: a drained embedding queue can leave pages unclassified

Using the committed pipeline harness with `maxNewPagesPerRun: 1`, a first run through fixture day two followed by repeated runs at the same time produced:

| Result after 38 runs | Count |
|---|---:|
| Pages | 38 |
| Embedded pages | 38 |
| Remaining embedding backlog | 0 |
| Pages with memberships | 1 |
| Pages with explicit exclusions | 0 |
| Embedded pages with neither membership nor exclusion | 37 |

`lib/pipeline.js:370` clears `needsEmbedding`; line 380 subsequently omits pages without events in the current overlap window. The existing incremental test checks that the embedding backlog drains, but does not assert classification completion.

Maintain durable work status through registry or intentional exclusion commit, then recompute affected historical days. Queue exhaustion must mean every eligible page reached a deliberate outcome. Topic-budget deferrals also need a durable classification retry path.

### Confirmed: sensitive routes bypass privacy exclusions

`lib/privacy.js:53` examines only the un-decoded pathname. Direct checks gave:

| Synthetic URL | Excluded |
|---|---|
| `https://example.test/login` | Yes |
| `https://example.test/#/login?token=FAKE` | No |
| `https://example.test/%6cogin` | No |

Inspect decoded route components consistently before capture and ingest. Avoid treating the default domain list as comprehensive protection. Separately sanitize stored and exported URLs; canonicalization currently does not remove the original raw URL from the record.

### Confirmed at buffer level: clear does not cancel an in-flight write

`lib/captureBuffer.js:45` snapshots pending rows before awaiting persistence. Its `clear()` only empties the Map. A controlled store with a delayed write reproduced a persisted row after buffer clear and storage wipe.

`background.js:341` uses buffer clear around deletion without waiting for the outstanding flush chain. This supports the deletion race finding; the complete live Chrome deletion lifecycle was not exercised in this review. Give deletion an exclusive barrier that prevents new writers and waits for or cancels existing capture, maintenance, and analysis work before wiping.

### Other issues found by code inspection

- **Final capture state:** `background.js:49` forwards only `message.capture`, ignoring the final flag. Text-free updates can remain in memory until a later flush. Persist terminal updates and flush before analysis.
- **Embedding-space invariants:** `lib/pipeline.js:328` trusts cached vectors, and the persisted embedding-space setting is a model name rather than a verified model/dimension contract. Reject invalid cached vectors and handle space changes explicitly.
- **Cluster merging:** `lib/registry.js:53` weights cosine by 0.7 and overlap by 0.3, with a merge threshold of 0.80. Production new clusters contain unmembered pages, so overlap is zero and this cluster-level merge branch cannot clear the threshold. Direct assignment to existing topics is a separate path and does operate. Revisit the matching contract before trusting long-term topic fragmentation behavior.
- **Changing chat pages:** `content/capture.js:126` re-extracts only an initially thin page, once. A conversation already over 200 characters does not get a fresh text capture merely because more turns arrive at the same URL. This limits the pipeline's planned chat-growth re-embedding behavior. Test a realistic multi-turn conversation with bounded capture updates.
- **Demo navigation:** Clicking Map from the recorded demo led to a permanent loading screen. The standalone map only boots in an extension context, and the demo's in-memory store is not passed to that page. Make the sample-data journey self-contained before using it for recruiting testers.

## Evidence and validation limits

- Current `npm test`: **21/21 test files passed**, including the populated-map test. The earlier populated-map contract bug is now covered; it should not be repeated as a current extension-wide crash finding.
- Inspected the recorded home screen in a browser and checked its Map navigation.
- Ran synthetic Node reproductions for backlog completion, page identity, sensitive routes, and the in-flight buffer write.
- Ollama was not listening on localhost port 11434. No fresh live-model validation, benchmark, or live pipeline end-to-end run was performed. The Chrome capture end-to-end suite was not rerun in this review.
- The committed August 25 validation reports ARI 0.996 on 200 ingested topical synthetic pages, with zero production-gated adjudicated clusters. That is useful fixture evidence, not evidence of equivalent accuracy on people's browsing. The separate adjudication recording demonstrates the protocol under a widened gate.

Before making quality claims, evaluate naturally overlapping subjects, query-addressed pages, growing chats, changing titles, ordinary navigation noise, repeated visits, and different capture coverage. Assess both topic quality and useful recovery. Two agreeing passes from the same model establish agreement; they do not independently establish correctness.

## Proposed path to a small pilot

| Stage | Deliverable | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Establish trust | Fix identity, backlog, route exclusions, and writer lifecycle | Focused regression checks pass; restart, pause, delete, and deferred-work scenarios leave the promised state |
| Complete one useful loop | Recent trail list → dated evidence → open an original page; basic trail search | A person can recover relevant material without help navigating the map |
| Make setup reviewable | Install/run checks, clear progress, useful empty and offline states, working sample demo | Observe and record setup time and first successful recovery on supported machines |
| Run a two-week pilot | Five participants in one recurring research workflow | Concrete recovery examples and voluntary use in both weeks |

Provisional pilot thresholds, chosen as decision aids rather than industry benchmarks: at least four of five participants complete a first recovery during onboarding, and at least three of five voluntarily recover useful material in both weeks. Record failed searches and wrong groupings as well as successes. Use local counters and participant-approved examples; raw browsing history need not be collected centrally.

If people inspect the visualization once but do not recover anything later, revisit the benefit before adding more analytics. If they repeatedly recover material but resent installation, prioritize packaging and inference cost. If they return for reflection instead, reconsider the primary experience using those observed moments.

The next conversation should start with a concrete incident: the last time you lost a useful page, context, or line of thought, what exactly would Cognitive Trails have needed to show you?
